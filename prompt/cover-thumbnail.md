# Cover thumbnail

How the cover image is made from 20 October 2026. scripts/engine/thumbnail.mjs
reads this file, gives the section for the site to the writer model with the
finished article, and the model returns a headline and an image prompt in the
same shape as the example below. The examples are the two covers David approved
on 20 October ("YES sooo much better"). They show the standard; their scenes are
not to be reused.

These are not David's thirty style prompt followed to the letter. That prompt
produced the first two covers, and he called them terrible. What he approved
came from prompts written like the ones here, so these are the standard.

## Rules for both sites

- Text on the image, on both sites. A headline of three to five words, the
  reader's benefit or the article's sharpest idea. Never the post title and
  never the keyword repeated: his own sample covers printed the title in big
  letters, and a cover laid out like his sample looks copied from it.
- No people, on either site, ever: no person, face, hand, arm, silhouette,
  reflection or shadow of one. This has been the rule from the start; on 22
  October a round of Strategic Marketer covers with people in them went live
  and was taken down within the hour.
- One concrete visual idea from the article. What kind of scene depends on the
  site, see its section: IdeoLab uses illustrated objects and metaphors,
  Strategic Marketer a photographed real place whose objects tell what just
  happened. Choose the composition each time. Not a laptop or a browser window
  by default, never a robot, a lightbulb or a dashboard of numbers.
- The headline is the only readable text. Every prompt ends by saying so, with
  the headline spelled out, and the engine reads the image back to check it.

## Scene rules

Added 22 October 2026, after David said the covers were "still terrible". Three
of the four Strategic Marketer covers of 20 and 21 October earned it. The style
was right; the scenes were not. What went wrong in each, so it is not repeated:

- "Save Time Before Spending": a basket of boxes with two wrenches, a curved
  track of cards, a brass gate, a tray and a stopwatch. Five ideas, none of them
  readable at a glance.
- "Save Time, Not Guesswork": the article said "buy more tools" and the cover
  showed a hammer, pliers and a wrench. Software tools are never drawn as hand
  tools.
- "Earn Buyer Trust": a row of tiles ending in a barred gate that reads as a jail
  door, on a background split down the right side by a visible seam.

So every scene now has to pass all of these:

- One hero doing one thing: one object or one tight group. At most three other
  small supporting pieces. If the scene needs a sentence with "and" to explain
  it, it is two scenes; keep the stronger one.
- A viewer understands what it says in two seconds, at the size of a phone card.
  The approved two do: gears driving bars up, a pencil line becoming a website.
- Nothing literal from a figure of speech: no hand tools for "tools", no baskets
  or carts for "buying", no keys and locks for "unlock", no puzzles, no
  rockets, no stopwatches or hourglasses for "time".
- Nothing that reads as negative or odd: no bars, cages, gates, chains, broken
  or melting things, nothing that looks like a prison, a trap or a warning.
- One continuous background, no panels, seams or split tones.
- The hook and the scene say the same thing. Write that one sentence first; if
  the picture does not show it, change the picture.
- Do not repeat a scene or a hook already used on this site.

## Site: ideolab

Style: bright, airy pastel SaaS 2.5D illustration, soft and premium. Rounded
floating objects and simple interface cards with soft shadows, luminous pastel
gradients (peach, baby blue, lavender, mint, white) with one clear medium blue
accent. Headline in a large bold rounded sans serif, deep navy. Objects and
interface pieces only, no people and no faces.

Approved example, "AI Text to Website Builder" article, headline "Your Website,
No Designer Needed":

A 16:9 blog thumbnail in a bright, airy pastel SaaS 2.5D illustration style. Right half: a large bold rounded sans-serif headline in deep navy on two lines, reading exactly "Your Website," on the first line and "No Designer Needed" on the second line, right of center with generous margins from every edge. Left half: a friendly oversized pastel yellow pencil leans diagonally and writes one soft wavy line on a small sheet of paper; the wavy line lifts off the paper as a glowing lavender ribbon that curls upward and folds itself into a small three-dimensional website standing like a tiny building, its floors made of stacked pastel website sections: a header bar, a picture block with a soft sun, a rounded blue button and a row of cards. From the roof of that little website building a white paper plane is taking off with a soft trail, as if the site is launching. Background: a soft gradient from peach to baby blue with a few blurred rounded shapes and small sparkles, gentle soft shadows. Palette: peach, baby blue, lavender, mint, white, with one clear medium blue accent. The only readable text anywhere in the image is the two-line headline, spelled exactly as given. No other words, letters, numbers, logos or watermarks, and nothing readable on the paper, the website or any other surface.

## Site: strategicmarketer

Changed 22 October 2026, twice. The covers of 20 October were still lifes of
cards, trays, gears and brass rails on ivory: premium, but they told no story,
and without the headline nobody could tell what any of them was about. The
first fix put people in them. That broke a rule that has stood from the start,
no people in any cover on either site, and those covers were taken down within
the hour. What stays from that attempt is the story. The people go.

So every Strategic Marketer cover is a small story told by a real place and
the things in it, with nobody there: the moment just after something happened,
or just before, where the objects show what it was. David's ICP for this site
(knowledge/strategicmarketer/icp-partner-edition.txt, section 24) gives the
stories worth telling: a prospect looking the business up and finding proof,
content that sounds like the real owner, a repeatable engine instead of random
posting, the owner no longer the bottleneck, offline credibility showing up
online. It also lists what to avoid: robots, neon AI graphics, glowing brain
visuals, overdesigned agency ads, generic stock footage.

The story comes first, in "says": what the picture shows and why it matters.
"A tailor's fitting room where a jacket hangs pinned and chalked on the dummy
while the paperwork on the desk behind it is already finished and closed,
because the owner's time goes to the client, not the admin."

- Where: a real business, from the article's own examples or the one the
  engine names. Its counter, workbench, treatment room, front door, shelf of
  orders, desk at closing time.
- The moment: the evidence of the result the headline names. Wrapped orders
  lined up by the door, ready. An after-care envelope waiting on the chair. A
  full appointment book. A finished job with the tools put away. The first
  coffee poured at opening with the day's work already laid out.
- A stranger can say in one sentence what just happened or is about to happen,
  at phone size. One hero object or one tight group, at most three small
  supporting pieces, all real objects a real business has. No invented
  gadgets, discs, buttons or devices.
- The story is told by things in the room, never by a screen. Nothing on a
  screen can be read, so a screen cannot carry the story. A phone or laptop can
  be a small prop at most; with every screen blank, a stranger should still
  get it.

How it looks:

- One continuous editorial photograph, edge to edge, in natural window light,
  a real business interior, calm and uncluttered, colour graded to the house
  palette: warm ivory and sand, sage teal, slate blue, small touches of brushed
  brass.
- No people at all: no person, face, hand, arm, silhouette, reflection or
  shadow of one. The image check rejects any.
- The headline is set over a calm, softly out of focus part of that same scene
  (a wall, a window, an empty stretch of counter) in an elegant high contrast
  serif, deep charcoal navy (ivory if that part is dark), with generous
  margins. Never describe the image as a left part and a right part or give the
  headline its own panel: on 22 October that made the image model put two
  photos side by side with a hard seam between them, twice in three tries.
- Vary the place and the hero object from cover to cover. Not every story is a
  desk or an envelope.
- Nothing readable on paper, signs, packaging, mugs, folders or clothing. A prop
  that invites a label (a "tax" folder, a menu, a shop sign) is left out or
  shown blank.
- No robots, glowing brains, neon, holograms, floating icons, arrows or charts.
  Nothing in the picture says "AI".

The palette and serif David approved on 20 October stay. The gears still life
("Make Every Marketing Dollar Work") is the record of them, not the model for
new scenes.

Example, written 22 October for "Before You Buy More Tools, Choose AI Solutions
That Save Time", headline "More Time With Clients":

A 16:9 blog thumbnail, one continuous editorial photograph running edge to edge, in soft natural morning window light, calm and uncluttered, colour graded to warm ivory, sand, sage teal and slate blue with small touches of brushed brass. A small tailor's fitting room with nobody in it, seen at eye level: toward the left of the frame, a slate blue jacket on a dressmaker's dummy, one sleeve pinned and marked with a line of pale chalk, a tape measure draped over its shoulder and a brass pincushion on the stand. Just behind it, a small wooden desk where the day's paperwork is already finished: a closed sage folder squared on a neat stack, a brass pen laid across it. The moment says: the fitting is where the owner's time goes now, the admin is done. Toward the right, the same room continues into a calm ivory wall softly out of focus with morning light on it, and the headline is set on that wall in an elegant high-contrast serif in deep charcoal navy on two lines, reading exactly "More Time" on the first line and "With Clients" on the second line, left aligned, with generous margins from every edge. One photograph, no panels, borders or split. No people, no hands, no faces, no silhouettes or reflections of anyone. The only readable text in the entire image is that headline, spelled exactly as given. No other words, letters, numbers, labels, logos or watermarks anywhere, including on the folder, the paper, the tape measure and the jacket.
