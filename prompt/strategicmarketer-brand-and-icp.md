# Strategic Marketer: brand, audience and claim rules

Read this together with `prompt/article-master-prompt.md`. That prompt is the writing
standard and it is deliberately brand independent. It says to resolve the brand,
product, service, audience and approved action from the supplied materials. For a
Strategic Marketer article, this file is those materials.

Everything below is taken from strategicmarketer.com as published on 28 September
2026. Where a fact you need is not here, leave it out or verify it on the site.
Never invent it, and never carry a fact across from an IdeoLab article.

## What Strategic Marketer is

Strategic Marketer helps established businesses identify, install and activate
practical AI systems built around their brand, knowledge and growth priorities.

The line the site leads with: stop experimenting with AI, start putting it to work.

The argument underneath it, in the site's own framing: the problem is not access to
AI, it is knowing what to actually do with it. Businesses are not ignoring AI. They
are stuck between interest and implementation.

## The Business Intelligence Agent

This is the foundation of everything Strategic Marketer sells, and most articles
will lead back to it.

Before AI can work for a business, it has to understand that business. The Business
Intelligence Agent is where that understanding is held:

1. Brand identity, meaning the name, the look, the values and how the business
   presents itself
2. Brand voice
3. The products, services and offers the business sells
4. Its customers and its positioning
5. A single source of truth for approved facts

What the site says goes wrong without it: inconsistent messaging from tool to tool,
incorrect assumptions stated confidently, off brand tone that does not sound like
the business, disconnected work that never compounds, and low value automation of
the wrong things.

What the site says follows once it is in place: consistent brand visibility, better
content produced faster, stronger customer follow up, sales enablement, automated
repetitive work, and centralised business knowledge.

## The approved offer and the only approved action

The offer is the **AI Business Assessment**. The action is to book it.

What the site promises from that assessment, and the only version of it an article
may state:

- A clear diagnosis of what is holding AI back in the business
- The two or three places AI would pay for itself first
- A prioritised roadmap the business keeps either way
- No pressure and no obligation, and if it is not a fit they say so

The assessment is described as one conversation. Do not invent a price, a duration,
a trial, a discount, a deadline, a guarantee or any availability claim. Do not offer
a call, a demo, a download or a newsletter in its place.

## How the work is described

The five stages on the site, in order: understand the growth, diagnose growth gaps,
prioritise where AI creates the greatest near term value, map the right combination
of technology, implementation, automation, training or managed support, then activate
the Business Intelligence Agent.

How they distinguish themselves, in their own four words: Build It, meaning their own
technology rather than repackaged third party tools. Fit It, shaped around how the
business already runs. Install It, activated inside the company rather than left as
advice. Train Your Team, so the people running it can do so confidently.

The contrast they draw: most consultants hand over a list of AI tools and leave.

## The two audiences

Write every article for one of these two, and say which in the frontmatter. Never
try to serve both in one piece.

**Side A, agencies and consultants.** Agency owners, marketing consultants and
fractional operators who sell services to businesses. The site has a For Agencies
section and a large share of its published testimonials come from agency owners
describing deals they closed. Their pressure is winning and keeping clients, and
delivering without the work routing through one person.

**Side B, established business owners.** Owners and operators of businesses that are
already running, across home services, healthcare, professional services, real
estate and ecommerce. The site states it has worked with businesses in all five in
the last ninety days. Their pressure is that AI is clearly useful to somebody and
they cannot tell what it should do inside their own company.

Side B is the audience the homepage is written for. Side A is the audience most of
the proof comes from. Both are real and both are served.

## Claim rules, which are stricter here than they look

The site carries a wall of testimonials with specific figures in them. A closed deal
at sixty thousand dollars. A setup fee of fourteen hundred and ninety seven dollars
with nine hundred and ninety seven a month. Three thousand seven hundred and fifty a
month from a dental implant specialist. Twenty three minutes to a first client.

These are real quotes from named customers on a published page. They are not
permission to promise those outcomes.

- Never present any of those figures as typical, expected or likely
- Never write a sentence that implies a reader will get a similar result
- Never restate a testimonial as though it were a measured study or an average
- If a specific result is used at all, attribute it as one customer's account and
  keep the surrounding sentences free of implied promise
- Prefer an illustrative worked example over a quoted result, and label it clearly
  as illustrative

The master prompt already bans invented statistics and unsupported outcome claims.
On this brand that rule carries more weight, because plausible looking numbers are
sitting on the site waiting to be misused.

## Vocabulary

Use: Business Intelligence Agent, AI Business Assessment, brand identity, brand
voice, source of truth, growth gaps, roadmap, implementation, activate, install.

Avoid: any phrasing that makes the product sound like a tool rather than a system
installed inside a business, and any phrasing that promises AI does the work on its
own. The whole positioning is that the business context comes first and the systems
follow.

## Links

Link to strategicmarketer.com pages only where the article genuinely leads there,
and keep the count low. The pages the site exposes in its own navigation are How We
Help, Our Approach, Industries, Results, About and For Agencies, plus the assessment
booking as the action.

Do not link to ideolab.ai from a Strategic Marketer article, do not mention IdeoLab,
and do not reuse an IdeoLab call to action. The two brands are written separately.
