CONTENT VELOCITY
ICP Marketing Public Document
External-facing ideal customer profile for marketing partners
PURPOSE
This document gives outside marketing partners a clear understanding of who Content Velocity is designed for, what those buyers care about, and how to speak to them. It intentionally excludes internal scoring, campaign data, proprietary research, detailed sales scripts, pricing logic, targeting rules, and operational playbooks.
Ideal Customer in One Sentence
Content Velocity is built for established business owners and expert-led brands that already have real value, customers, experience, or market credibility — but need a more consistent, authentic online presence without becoming full-time content creators or marketing technicians.
The Core Customer
• Runs an established business, practice, consultancy, service company, product brand, or expert-led organization.
• Already has a real offer, real expertise, real customers, or a proven reputation.
• Knows that visibility, trust, education, and authority influence buying decisions.
• Feels that the business is stronger in real life than it appears online.
• Wants consistent content, but does not have the time, team, or system to produce it reliably.
• Values authenticity and wants content to reflect the real business rather than sound generic or artificial.
Simple Marketing Shorthand
THEY ARE
Credible but under-visible. Established but inconsistent online. Busy, practical, and protective of their reputation. They want leverage from content without turning marketing into another full-time job.

What They Are Struggling With
Inconsistent Content
They know they should publish more consistently, but content repeatedly falls behind day-to-day business priorities.
Online Credibility Gap
Their reputation, expertise, or customer value is stronger than what prospects can see when they search or research the business online.
Too Much Dependence on Referrals
Word-of-mouth may be strong, but growth is limited when people outside the existing network cannot easily discover or evaluate the business.
Time and Execution Pressure
The owner is often the knowledge source and the bottleneck. They do not want to personally write, record, edit, schedule, and manage everything.
Generic Marketing Frustration
They may have tried agencies, freelancers, or AI tools and felt that the output did not sound like them or understand the business.
Fragmented Marketing
They may have a website, social channels, videos, articles, or ads, but the pieces are not working together as a consistent content presence.
What They Want Instead
• A consistent online presence that makes the business easier to find, understand, and trust.
• Content that reflects their expertise, personality, brand, and point of view.
• A practical way to turn what they already know into useful articles, social content, and video.
• More authority and visibility without chasing trends, virality, or constant posting for its own sake.
• Less manual effort and fewer disconnected marketing tasks.
• A process where they can remain the expert and decision-maker without personally producing every asset.
What Matters Most to Them
Authenticity
The content must feel true to the business and its real voice.
Trust
They want prospects to encounter proof of expertise before the sales conversation.
Simplicity
They do not want another complicated marketing system that adds more work.
Consistency
They want content to keep moving even when the owner is busy.
Control
They want strategic visibility into what is being said and how the brand is represented.
Business Relevance
They care about content that supports real customer understanding and action, not vanity metrics alone.

Best-Fit Business Types
The ICP is defined more by business maturity and the importance of trust than by one specific industry.
Professional Services & Experts
Established consultants, advisors, coaches, practitioners, specialists, and professional service providers with proven expertise.
Local & Regional Businesses
Businesses where local reputation, education, trust, and search visibility influence who customers choose.
High-Value B2B Services
Businesses where a relatively small number of clients, projects, or engagements can be economically meaningful.
Product & E-Commerce Brands
Established product companies that need consistent storytelling, education, and multi-channel brand visibility.
Education & Specialty Businesses
Training, education, specialty instruction, and expertise-driven businesses where explaining the value clearly matters.
Founder-Led Brands
Businesses where the founder’s knowledge, story, perspective, or reputation is an important part of why customers trust the company.
Common Buyer Mindset
• “I know we need to be more visible online.”
• “I do not have enough time to create content consistently.”
• “Our online presence does not fully reflect how good the business really is.”
• “I want content that actually sounds like us.”
• “I do not want to manage another complicated marketing project.”
• “I want a system and support, not more random ideas.”
General Buying Characteristics
• They are practical rather than trend-driven.
• They are usually willing to invest in marketing when the business case is clear.
• They may be curious about AI, skeptical of AI, or already disappointed by generic AI output — but AI itself is not the primary reason they buy.
• They respond better to clarity, authenticity, implementation, and useful business outcomes than to hype.
• They want to participate enough to keep the content accurate and on-brand, but they do not want to be responsible for every production step.
Not the Ideal Fit
• Pre-revenue businesses or people still trying to decide what they sell.
• Prospects with no proven offer, customers, expertise, or business substance to amplify.
• People primarily looking for the cheapest self-serve content tool.
• People expecting guaranteed leads, instant sales, viral growth, or fully automated marketing with no involvement.
• People who do not value brand accuracy, review, or business-specific content.
Public Messaging Guidance
Marketing should speak to the customer’s existing credibility and the gap between the quality of the business and the consistency of its online presence.
Lead With
• Established expertise that is not visible enough online.
• The need for consistent content without making the owner the content department.
• Authentic brand voice and business-specific content.
• Visibility, authority, trust, and consistency.
• A practical content system rather than scattered one-off marketing tasks.
• Human control, accuracy, and content that supports real business goals.
Avoid Leading With
• AI hype or technical jargon.
• Promises of unlimited content, instant results, or fully automated marketing.
• Virality, follower growth, or influencer-style positioning.
• Content volume as the main value proposition.
• Guaranteed leads, rankings, sales, or conversions.
• Messaging that makes the customer feel old, behind, uninformed, or technically inadequate.
Tone and Creative Direction
Tone
Direct, experienced, plainspoken, reassuring, practical, confident, and businesslike.
Creative Feel
Human, credible, founder-led, natural, clear, and grounded in real business situations.
Emotional Center
“My business is real and credible. I need my online presence to reflect that without adding more work to my plate.”
Primary Outcome
Become more findable, trusted, and consistent online while preserving the identity and expertise that already make the business valuable.
Agency Summary
THE PUBLIC ICP
Target established owners and expert-led businesses that already have something worth amplifying. Their problem is not a lack of business substance; it is that their expertise, credibility, products, and customer value are not being translated into consistent online content. Speak to authenticity, visibility, trust, consistency, and execution relief. Avoid hype, shortcuts, and promises that make the offer sound like generic AI content or a lead-generation gimmick.
