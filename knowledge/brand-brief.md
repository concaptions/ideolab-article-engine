## What IdeoLab is

IdeoLab is an AI business creation workspace designed to help people move from
business information and ideas to finished marketing work without constantly
rebuilding the project in separate tools.

The marketing story: Research, Understand, Create, Repurpose, Publish, Measure,
Improve, Automate.

It can begin from many kinds of real business material (a website, document,
video, image, audio recording, customer review, research finding, written brief,
existing social post, or a simple idea) and turn that material into useful
marketing outputs.

Three stories to tell:

1. Continuity. The material that informs a project stays with the project, so
   the next article, presentation, graphic, social post, image, site or campaign
   does not begin from zero.
2. Review and refinement. Built for ongoing creation, not one-shot output. People
   review direction, improve the work, create variations, and keep using strong
   assets later.
3. One business idea becoming many assets. Research becomes a presentation. A
   video becomes social content. Real photos become posts. Reviews become
   messaging. A website becomes campaign research.

## Who we write for

Every article is written for ONE side. Set `audience_side` in the frontmatter.

### Side A: Agency and growth operators

Agency owners, marketers, consultants, fractional growth leaders, specialized
service providers, commercially experienced operators.

Their problem: they know how to create value, but client acquisition, service
delivery, research, content production and growth still depend too heavily on
time, people and manual effort.

Pain hierarchy (in order): owner bottleneck; inconsistent growth activity;
service ceiling; fulfillment economics; under-monetized client relationships;
tool overload without implementation; too much customization; shiny-object
distraction; AI opportunity without commercial clarity.

They want: more capacity, more client value, more recurring revenue, easier
execution, greater leverage.

Rules for Side A: respect existing experience, never teach basic
entrepreneurship. Lead with leverage, client value, capacity, margins, recurring
revenue and execution. Do not make the quantity of capabilities the value
proposition. AI is the mechanism, not the story. Avoid promises of automatic
clients, effortless revenue or "no selling required". If they already have
systems that work, IdeoLab is additive, not a replacement.

### Side B: Business owners and in-house operators

Established B2B owners, experts, local operators, professional firms, product
brands, time-poor marketing decision-makers.

Their problem: the business has real value, expertise, customers, products or
reputation, but marketing is inconsistent, fragmented, under-produced or
disconnected from the quality of the real business.

Pain hierarchy (in order): marketing inconsistency; digital credibility gap;
time scarcity; generic AI frustration; agency and freelancer friction; referral
dependence; asset underuse; no feedback loop; too many disconnected tools.

They want: more visibility, credibility, consistency, marketing capacity, and
less dependence on the owner doing everything manually.

Rules for Side B: lead with the business problem (visibility, credibility,
inconsistency, time, execution burden). Never make them feel technically
inadequate or behind. The goal is not becoming an influencer or going viral.
Emphasize authenticity, usefulness, consistency, business relevance. Treat
existing business knowledge and assets as valuable raw material. Do not promise
fully autonomous marketing or zero involvement. Avoid "AI content at scale"
language. Connect marketing to trust, customer understanding, visibility, sales
support and customer action.

### The common denominator

The strongest IdeoLab buyer already has something worth amplifying. They do not
need AI to invent their value. They need a better way to turn existing value
into visible, useful, repeatable marketing.

Final rule: make the buyer feel understood before making IdeoLab feel impressive.

## Words and claims

Prefer: leverage, capacity, consistency, credibility, visibility, business
context, authenticity, client value, recurring revenue, less manual work, more
useful output from existing assets, professional marketing without proportional
overhead, control without being the bottleneck.

Never write: one platform that does everything, push-button AI, fully automated
marketing, set it and forget it, unlimited content, no effort required,
guaranteed customers, guaranteed revenue, guaranteed rankings, go viral, AI does
everything, replace your entire team, anyone can do it, make money online,
business in a box.

Public claim rules (from the source of truth):

- Lead with the customer problem, job and outcome, not a product control.
- Describe customer-visible capabilities and benefits. No behind-the-scenes
  technology talk.
- No absolute superiority claims.
- Do not present dynamic counts, network lists, limits or availability as
  permanent (no "200+ integrations", no fixed "X minutes" promises).
- Acknowledge that specialist competitors can be excellent at their core job.
  Position IdeoLab around the connected business workflow.
- Do not present unreleased capabilities as available.
- Never invent customer results, performance lifts, savings, testimonials or
  market share. Every statistic links to a real source or is labeled illustrative.

## How to build an article (from the source of truth, section 16)

1. Identify the exact customer job. Start with the problem, not a feature list.
2. Choose ONE relevant IdeoLab capability from `knowledge/feature-index.md`, then
   read its full section in the source of truth for use cases and FAQs.
3. Choose the buyer stage (Awareness, Consideration, Decision). Consideration
   content explains category tradeoffs before introducing IdeoLab. Decision
   content handles objections.
4. Choose one primary search intent and one secondary intent.
5. Choose one marketing angle (pain, time, context-switch, existing assets,
   authenticity, evidence before opinion, one source many formats, review
   control, consistency, repurposing, small-team leverage, agency scalability,
   local business, closed-loop learning, old way vs connected way, contrarian,
   proof, control). Vary the angle from the previous article.
6. Include at least one detailed use case and one broader workflow example.
7. Use competitor context only when it helps the buyer, and acknowledge what the
   alternative does well.
8. End on the business outcome and the next action, not on AI novelty.

## Format numbers David confirmed (Slack, 22 Sep 2026)

- One article per day; he may move to two per day, one per side.
- 1,400 to 1,800 words.
- 6 to 8 body sections, 4 to 7 FAQs.
- 2 to 3 links to IdeoLab product pages.
- Minimum 1 to 2 external citations to real sources.
- One cover image per article, generated with the Kie key in `.env`. Describe
  the intended cover in `cover_brief` first: subject, setting, mood. No text in
  the image, no fake screenshots, no fake results, no logos.
- No brand voice document yet. The ICP is the voice guide.
